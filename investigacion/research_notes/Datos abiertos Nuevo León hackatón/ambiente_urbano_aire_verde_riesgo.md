# Ambiente urbano AMM (Monterrey): aire, verde/arbolado, calor, riesgo, movilidad-emisiones — inventario de datos ground-level (corte 2026-10-14)

Notation: "[verified 2026-10-14]" = URL/endpoint opened directly by the researcher on that date (curl/fetch). Everything else is cited to the page shown; where only a search-result summary was available (not the full page), it is flagged "(search summary)". Figures are dated by their source period.

## 1. Air quality — SIMA, SINAICA, OpenAQ, low-cost sensors, emission sources/inventories, rankings, health

### Takeaway
SIMA (state network, 15 fixed + 2 mobile stations, 6 criteria pollutants + meteorology, hourly) is the only reference-grade ground network. Its real-time data is reachable through undocumented JSON endpoints and its history through monthly PDF reports. The cleanest historical, machine-readable route is the national SINAICA mirror (15 Monterrey station IDs) through the `rsinaica` R package. Official 2025 annual averages fell (PM10 49 µg/m³, PM2.5 18.9 µg/m³), but independent counts still show more than half of 2025's days over the norm. No published, up-to-date emissions inventory exists: the federal 2023-base inventory was announced as concluded on 30-Sep-2026, but no figures have been released. Low-cost sensor and health-impact data are thin and outdated, which is a clear gap.

### Inferences
- The official "historic improvement" and the independent "more than half of 2025's days dirty" are not contradictory. They use different metrics (annual mean vs. daily or 12-h exceedance counts under tightening NOM-025 phases). A hackathon tool that recomputes exceedances transparently from hourly SINAICA data under each norm phase would settle a live public dispute.
- The SIMA JSON endpoints are fragile and undocumented (single station, no history). The most robust stack is SINAICA via `rsinaica` for history plus SIMA JSON for "now". Data completeness (PM2.5 only ~81% valid days network-wide) must be handled explicitly.
- The pollutant mix differs by zone: PM10 and O3 dominate in the west (García/Misión San Juan, San Pedro), while PM2.5 hotspots are in the north and east (San Nicolás, Juárez). This suggests distinct sources (quarries and dust in the west vs. industry, refinery and traffic in the east) worth exploring with wind-rose and source-direction analysis from SIMA meteorology.
- The federal 2023 inventory being "concluded but unpublished" is a transparency gap. A team could prepare a pipeline to ingest it, or file transparency requests.

### Gaps
- No API documentation for SIMA, and no official bulk historical CSV download found on aire.nl.gob.mx (history is PDF-only there; use SINAICA).
- OpenAQ coverage of Monterrey stations is unconfirmed (needs an API key). The number of active PurpleAir sensors in the AMM was not counted.
- No 2025–2026 numbers by source were found for PM2.5/PM10/SO2/NOx (the 2023-base inventory is not public). The 2018 PIGECA inventory tables were not retrieved.
- Monterrey's IQAir 2025 annual PM2.5 and its current Latin American rank were not obtained (rate-limited). A recent WHO or PAHO ranking for 2018 or later was not found.
- Status of Aire CIE (UANL) and the Tec low-cost network in 2026 is unknown.

## 2. Green areas & trees — m²/hab, park inventories, tree censuses, ANPs, Río Santa Catarina, green corridors

### Takeaway
Green-area-per-capita figures for the AMM range from 3.9 to 7.75 m²/hab depending on method and year (all below WHO's 9 and the UN's 15). They are unequal: San Pedro ~13 m²/hab vs. the Monterrey centro/norte ~2.6–3.5, and peripheral AGEBs ~2 m²/hab. The AMM lost ~508 ha of urban green between 2000 and 2019, mostly in Juárez, Monterrey, Apodaca and Guadalupe. **No municipal georeferenced tree inventory was found for any AMM municipality**, which is the clearest data gap. Protected-area polygons and sizes are well documented.

### Cited Findings
- Sedesu 2017: AMM average **3.9 m²/hab** vs. the UN's 15, a 74% deficit — [Milenio](https://www.milenio.com/estados/falta-74-area-verde-zona-metropolitana)

### Inferences
- A per-municipality, per-AGEB "m² of green per inhabitant" is the most-cited indicator, but each study uses a different definition. A reproducible open calculation (OSM/INEGI Marco Geoestadístico + Censo 2020 + a ground-truthed park layer), with method published, would be a durable contribution. "Green equity vs. marginación (CONAPO IMU 2020)" at AGEB level has only been done partially, for children and select AGEBs (Casillas-Zapata 2022).
- Tree-level data is the biggest hole. A crowdsourced or i-Tree-style census app for one municipality (San Pedro has an academic baseline; Monterrey centro/norte have the worst deficits) is high-impact and feasible in a hackathon.
- Bosques Ciudadanos reports "trees planted" without survival or geolocation. A tracking or verification layer is an obvious accountability product.

### Gaps
- No official municipal park or tree inventory datasets found for Monterrey, San Pedro, Guadalupe, San Nicolás, Apodaca or Escobedo (they may exist internally or via transparency requests; datos.monterrey.gob.mx is a JS app whose content could not be verified).
- No ONU-Hábitat CPI municipal report for NL with green per-capita values was found online.
- Areas for Parque Fundidora, Parque Río La Silla and Parque Ecológico La Pastora, and the Parques y Vida Silvestre park inventory, were not retrieved (parquesyvidasilvestre domain did not resolve).
- Exact current Cerro de las Mitras protection (beyond "Sierra Las Mitras" state ANP, 3,357 ha) was not verified separately.

## 3. Urban heat — UHI studies, heat-related mortality, heat vulnerability maps

### Takeaway
UHI evidence for Monterrey is municipal-level and qualitative-to-semi-quantitative: the Tec Observatorio de Ciudades names San Nicolás, Escobedo, Apodaca, Guadalupe and Monterrey as most intense, and UANL's 2025 heat-index study from SIMA stations finds the east (Juárez, San Nicolás) worst during the 2023–2024 heat waves. Heat deaths were 102 in 2023, 14 in 2024 and 2 in 2025 in NL. **No official colonia-level heat vulnerability map was found**, and Monterrey's 2022 Chief Heat Officer office has unclear current status.

### Cited Findings
- Tec de Monterrey Observatorio de Ciudades: San Nicolás, Escobedo, Apodaca, Guadalupe and Monterrey have the highest UHI intensity, while Santiago and much of San Pedro stay cooler thanks to vegetation. Concrete vs. vegetated zones differ by up to 9 °C. An El Universal field walk measured 36 °C in colonia Industrial (north Monterrey), ~5 °C above the rest of the AMM — [Vanguardia/El Universal](https://vanguardia.com.mx/noticias/afirman-que-el-calor-que-sofoca-a-millones-de-habitantes-de-monterrey-no-es-unicamente-consecuencia-del-clima-IB22545638) (search summary)
- UANL (Instituto de Ingeniería Civil, Geomatics), Zapata Wah, Yépez Rincón & Velastegui-Montoya, "Spatiotemporal Heat Index Assessment in the Monterrey Metropolitan Area during Heat Wave occurrence", ISPRS Annals X-3/W3-2025 (published 2026). Method: SIMA T/RH interpolated with IDW plus SMN satellite temperature imagery. Results: 2023 (week 25) max HI 36.71 °C at the NE, NTE and SE2 stations vs. 33.63 °C in the west/centre (SO, SO2, CE, NO); 2024 (week 21) HI 37.92 °C at SE, SE2 and NE3 vs. 31–33 °C in the west. The eastern AMM (Juárez, San Nicolás) is most affected. The paper also cites an AMM record high of 48 °C (SMN), daily means >28 °C on ~10 days a year, the urban core ~5 °C warmer (Vivid Economics 2022), and **102 deaths due to extreme heat in 2023** (DGE 2023; CENAPRECE 2024). It also notes SIMA weather stations were installed in phases from 1992 to 2020 — [ISPRS Annals (PDF)](https://isprs-annals.copernicus.org/articles/X-3-W3-2025/165/2026/isprs-annals-X-3-W3-2025-165-2026.pdf) [verified text]
- Heat-season deaths in NL: 2023 season, 102; 2024, 14; 2025 (weeks 12–40), **2 deaths** and 103 cases (5th nationally: 59 heat stroke, 44 dehydration) — [Milenio](https://amp.milenio.com/comunidad/nuevo-leon-reporta-dos-muertes-temporada-calor-2025) (search summary). In June 2023, NL had 64 of 112 national heat deaths at that cutoff; the viral "850 deaths in two weeks" claim was rejected by the state and rated false — [Telemundo](https://www.telemundo.com/noticias/noticias-telemundo/clima/mexico-reporta-112-muertes-por-la-ola-de-calor-y-mas-de-1500-personas-rcna91756); [Telemundo fact-check](https://www.telemundo.com/noticias/amp/rcna108068)
- Official source for heat morbidity and mortality: the weekly "Informe Semanal de Daños a la Salud por Temperaturas Naturales Extremas" (DGE/Secretaría de Salud), state-level only — referenced in [Milenio](https://www.milenio.com/comunidad/nuevo-leon-reporta-dos-muertes-temporada-calor-2025) (search summary)
- Monterrey created Mexico's first extreme-heat office and appointed Chief Heat Officer Surella Segú (Apr-2022, Arsht-Rock / City Champions for Heat Action, initial USD 50k from the Rockefeller Foundation). She stated ~70% of the city experiences the UHI effect, with low-income colonias most exposed — [Expansión, Jun-2022](https://politica.expansion.mx/estados/2022/06/10/oficina-de-calor-extremo-monterrey); [Cool Coalition](https://coolcoalition.org/?p=4873); [BRINK](https://www.brinknews.com/chief-heat-officers-an-emergency-response-to-urban-heat/) (search summaries)
- Remote-sensing UHI comparison of 10 Mexican cities including Monterrey (Manzanilla, 2022 thesis, CICY) — [CICY repository (PDF)](https://cicy.repositorioinstitucional.mx/jspui/bitstream/1003/2900/1/2022-09-01-Manzanilla_Ulises-Islas-de-calor.pdf)
- National municipal-level heat-mortality vulnerability index for Mexico (Schwarz et al. 2024, *Environment International*) — [PDF](https://heathealth.info/wp-content/uploads/1-s2.0-S0160412024008183-main.pdf) (search summary)
- SIMA's Aug-2026 report states a heat wave ("onda de calor") persisted all of August 2026 — [SIMA Reporte Agosto 2026](https://aire.nl.gob.mx/docs/reportes/mensuales/2026/08_Reporte_Agosto_2026.pdf) [verified]

### Inferences
- The only dense ground temperature network is SIMA (15 stations, hourly T/RH via SINAICA and `api_meteo.php`). A colonia-level heat vulnerability index (exposure from SIMA plus Landsat LST, sensitivity from Censo 2020 age 65+ / IMU marginación, adaptive capacity from green area and AC access) would be new for Monterrey. The Juárez PCA index offers a template (see §7).
- Heat mortality is reported only at state level and weekly, so a hackathon can at best correlate state cases with SIMA heat-index series. Small-area mortality would need INEGI death microdata (EDR) by municipality.

### Gaps
- No official heat vulnerability or UHI map (state or municipal) found. The Tec Observatorio de Ciudades map itself was not located.
- No excess-mortality study for the 2023 heat wave in the AMM found.
- Current status and outputs of Monterrey's heat office (post-2024 administration) are unknown.

## 4. Environmental risk — atlases, floods, landslides, quarries, hillside sprawl

### Takeaway
Risk data is fragmented and mostly PDF. The state atlas dates from 2012. Monterrey's municipal atlas in force is from 2009, and its 2024 update only reached public consultation. San Pedro has the best online atlas (an interactive app plus annex PDFs). Major floods (Alex 2010, Hanna 2020, Alberto 2024) are well documented in the press, with rainfall and fatality numbers, but there is no consolidated open flood-event dataset.

### Cited Findings
- State atlas: the most recent Atlas de Riesgo of the NL government dates from **2012** (per a Jul-2024 legislative post after Storm Alberto). - Monterrey: the atlas in force was published in 2009. Cabildo approved a public consultation for a new "Atlas de Peligros y Riesgos de la Ciudad de Monterrey" in 2024 — [Dictamen (PDF)](https://monterrey.gob.mx/pdf/dictamenes_cabildo/2024/Dictamen_mediante_el_cual_se_aprueba_la_Consulta_Pública_para_la_Expedición_del_Atlas_de_Peligros_y_Riesgos_de_la_Ciudad_de_Monterrey..pdf). - **Alberto (Jun-2024)**: at least 4 deaths in NL (3 electrocutions, including in Allende and El Carmen, and a 15-year-old drowned in the Río La Silla, Monterrey) — [Crónica](https://www.cronica.com.mx/nacional/tormenta-tropical-alberto-deja-4-muertos-nuevo-leon.html). Rainfall was 300–372 mm in Santa Catarina, Santiago and Monterrey and 200–274 mm in San Pedro and San Nicolás (Conagua via El Imparcial). The Río Santa Catarina reached ~80% capacity, overflowed and collapsed a Morones Prieto express lane, and dams refilled — [El Imparcial, 21-Jun-2024](https://www.elimparcial.com/mexico/2024/06/21/tormenta-tropical-alberto-termina-con-la-sequia-nuevo-leon-el-estado-mas-beneficiado/); [UnoTV](https://www.unotv.com/estados/nuevo-leon/tormenta-tropical-alberto-2024-imagenes-de-rios-desbordados-tras-su-paso-por-nuevo-leon/); [Vanguardia](https://vanguardia.com.mx/noticias/se-termina-el-desabasto-en-monterrey-sube-hasta-en-105-el-nivel-de-las-presas-de-nuevo-leon-tras-la-tormenta-tropical-alberto-JG12367428) (search summaries)
- **Hanna (Jul-2020)**: ~564 mm in 48 h in Monterrey (federal Protección Civil). The federal toll was 3 dead and 4 missing across Tamaulipas, NL and Coahuila, vs. 4 and 6 in early state figures. - **Alex (Jul-2010)**: 446.5 mm in 24 h in Monterrey and >616 mm in 60 h (Conagua) — [Expansión, 02-Jul-2010](https://expansion.mx/nacional/2010/07/02/el-huracan-ale-deja-mas-lluvia-que-gilberto-conagua). NL took MXN 6,507 M of Banobras debt for reconstruction — [Expansión, 13-Jul-2011](https://expansion.mx/nacional/2011/07/13/nuevo-leon-encara-una-deuda-de-20-anos-por-el-paso-del-huracan-alex). 11 deaths in Mexico — [Los Tiempos](https://www.lostiempos.com/actualidad/mundo/20100704/suben-once-muertos-mexico-paso-del-huracan-alex) (search summaries)
- Quarries as a risk and land-use issue: 7 closures announced, partly because they sat inside ANPs (Santa Catarina, Santiago, Escobedo, García) — [Excélsior](https://www.excelsior.com.mx/nacional/samuel-garcia-cierre-pedreras-nuevo-leon-contaminacion/1747802) (search summary)

### Inferences
- A "flood memory" dataset (geocoded events from Alex, Hanna and Alberto news and Protección Civil reports, crossed with arroyo and river channels, the INEGI hydrographic network and atlas hazard zones) does not exist publicly and would be a good hackathon deliverable.
- Hillside urbanization (Sierra Madre/Cumbres, Las Mitras, Cerro de la Silla) can be tracked against the ANP polygons listed in §2. A ground-level complement would be municipal construction permits (licencias de construcción) via transparency requests.

### Gaps
- No machine-readable (SHP/GeoJSON) state or Monterrey risk-atlas layers found. The 2012 state atlas file location was not found.
- No landslide inventory for the cerros (Loma Larga, Cerro de la Campana, Chipinque slopes) was found.
- No consolidated official count of quarries operating in the AMM (would need the Procuraduría Ambiental registry via transparency request).
- Final economic damage figures for Hanna and Alberto were not found.

## 6. Noise, waste (SIMEPRODE), illegal dumps

### Takeaway
Noise has legal limits but no measured open dataset or map. Waste is centralized at SIMEPRODE (>4,000 t/day landfilled, ~14% separated for recycling). The state's new "División Ambiental" reports enforcement counts (253 clandestine dumps closed in 2025) only as press-release aggregates, with no geolocated public data.

### Cited Findings
- Noise: a 2022 reform to the state Environmental Law caps residential-zone emissions at 55 dB (06:00–22:00) and 50 dB (22:00–06:00) — [HCNL, Sep-2022](https://www.hcnl.gob.mx/sala_de_prensa/2022/09/aprueba_congreso_establecer_limites_al_ruido.php) (search summary). A "90 dB average daytime" claim for Monterrey appears in an opinion column without a cited method, so it is unverified — [Vanguardia (opinion)](https://vanguardia.com.mx/opinion/monterrey-la-ciudad-que-grita-OE15449733)
- Waste: almost all AMM municipal solid waste goes to SIMEPRODE's final disposal site, with **>4,000 t/day landfilled** and only ~14% separated for recycling — [Ciencia UANL, Galera 135, Jan-2026 (PDF)](https://cienciauanl.uanl.mx/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/GALERA_135_OPINION.pdf) (search summary). A 2009 SIMEPRODE presentation cited ~5,000 t/day — [Global Methane (PDF)](https://development.globalmethane.org/documents/events_land_20090127_techtrans_padilla.pdf). A Banobras/Fonadin project (MXN 10,153 M) aims to extend landfill life to 30 years — [Proyectos México (PDF)](https://www.proyectosmexico.gob.mx/wp-content/cache/tmp/pdf_sostenbilidad/ES_81618_SOS.pdf) (search summaries). UANL CIE has a solid-waste note — [cie.uanl.mx (PDF)](https://cie.uanl.mx/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Residuos-solidos-urbanos.pdf)
- Illegal dumps and enforcement: Aug–Sep 2025 saw 87 clandestine dumps detected, 39 people detained, >80,000 kg of garbage and 19,800 kg of tires recovered, 140 inspections, 63 suspensions and 577,000 m² of public space recovered — [Gob. NL CP/1241/2025](https://www.nl.gob.mx/sites/default/files/repositorio/Dependencias/Secretar%C3%ADa%20de%20Medio%20Ambiente/Noticias/20250922_01_anuncian_modernizacion_historicadelsima.docx) [verified]. The full 2025 tally: **253 clandestine dumps closed** (many in rivers and arroyos with open burning), ~150 detained, 46 vehicles seized, ~300,000 t of garbage recovered, >167,000 kg of tires, ~150,000 kg of recyclables, >85,000 t sent to SIMEPRODE, 380 Procuraduría inspections, ~200 suspensions, 130 criminal complaints, and >375 fires attended by Protección Civil. 12 more dumps were suspended in the first week of Jan-2026 — [Gob. NL CP/0032/2026](https://www.nl.gob.mx/sites/default/files/repositorio/Dependencias/Secretar%C3%ADa%20de%20Medio%20Ambiente/Noticias/20260112_01_balance_medio_ambiente.docx) [verified]
- Odors: INECC and the state carried out an evaluation of bad odors in the AMM (Sep-2026) — [Gob. NL (docx)](https://www.nl.gob.mx/sites/default/files/repositorio/Dependencias/Secretar%C3%ADa%20de%20Medio%20Ambiente/Noticias/20260903_10_atender_malos_olores.docx) (search summary)

### Inferences
- Open burning at illegal dumps is a recognized PM source. A citizen-report and geolocation map of dumps and burning, cross-referenced with nearby SIMA PM2.5 spikes and arroyos, is a concrete, policy-aligned hackathon idea, since the state already runs a División Ambiental that could consume it.
- Noise is a near-blank field: a phone-sensor (dB) crowdsourcing map would be first-of-its-kind locally.

### Gaps
- No geolocated dataset of clandestine dumps, SIMEPRODE per-municipality tonnage series, or noise measurements was found (likely transparency-request-only via PNT).

## 7. Existing dashboards, projects and hackathon results to build on

### Takeaway
There is no documented Monterrey hackathon winner that crossed environmental datasets such as heat × marginación or green × marginación. The reusable assets are mostly air-quality data plumbing (rsinaica, the Cómo Vamos IME repo, AQICN, Aire CIE) and a handful of academic equity and heat studies. Colonia-level heat-vulnerability indices exist for Ciudad Juárez and CDMX and can serve as methodological templates.

### Cited Findings
- **Cómo Vamos NL – Índice de Monitoreo de Estaciones (IME)**: Python plus a CSV of valid days per station × pollutant, Oct-2025 to Sep-2026 — [GitHub](https://github.com/EvaluacionInvestigacion-CVNL/indice-monitoreo-estaciones-gcv) [verified]
- **rsinaica** (R) and **Sinaica.jl** (Julia) data clients — [diegovalle/rsinaica](https://github.com/diegovalle/rsinaica); [lethanix/Sinaica.jl](https://github.com/lethanix/Sinaica.jl) [verified via gh]. ### Inferences
- Highest-leverage, lowest-duplication hackathon problems, ranked by data readiness:
  1. An open SIMA/SINAICA archive and API, plus an "exceedance under each NOM phase" calculator and a station-uptime tracker extending Cómo Vamos' IME.
  2. A colonia/AGEB heat vulnerability index (SIMA T/RH + Censo 2020 + green area + marginación), modeled on the Juárez method.
  3. A green-equity map (m²/hab by AGEB vs. marginación) with an open, documented method.
  4. A tree and planting tracker with survival and geolocation (Bosques Ciudadanos accountability, a municipal tree census pilot).
  5. A geolocated illegal-dump and open-burning report map linked to PM2.5.
  6. A flood-event memory layer (Alex/Hanna/Alberto) over arroyo channels and atlas zones.
- Because ground-level open data for trees, noise, dumps and floods is largely absent, team projects in these areas will need crowdsourcing or transparency requests (PNT) as part of the scope. That is itself a valid "gap-filling" deliverable.

### Gaps
- No Monterrey/NL hackathon winner or public dashboard crossing heat, green or air with marginación was found. Codeando México or past NL government hackathon archives were not located.
- The Tec Observatorio de Ciudades and Hub de Ciudades, WRI México Monterrey projects, and IMCO's Índice de Competitividad Urbana environmental sub-indicators for Monterrey were not retrieved in this pass.
